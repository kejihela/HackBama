CAMPUSLOOP DEMO SCRIPT (about 4 minutes, roughly 600 spoken words)
==================================================================

Lines in [brackets] are what to do on screen. Everything else is what you say.


INTRO (0:00 - 0:20)
-------------------
[Landing page with the campus cards]

"Hi, we're Team BARK, and this is CampusLoop: a marketplace only for verified
college students, where you can buy, sell, or trade textbooks, dorm gear, game
tickets and rides, often without any cash changing hands. I'll show you five
things: how campuses join, how listings are verified, escrow, chat safety, and
the feature we're proudest of, the trade loop."


1. CREATING A CAMPUS: MULTI-TENANT, CROSS-CAMPUS, CAMPUS-VERIFIED (0:20 - 1:05)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[Open /admin -> Campuses -> fill in the new-campus form]

"CampusLoop is built as SaaS. One deployment serves every university, and each
campus is its own tenant with its own logo, colors, categories and safe meetup
spots. Launching a new school takes one form: no code, no redeploy."

[Submit, then go back to the landing page and show the new campus card]

"And it's live. Every campus is locked to its own .edu email domains, so only
real students from that school can sign in. That's what we mean by
campus-verified."

[Use the campus switcher in the top bar / show a Game Day listing from Auburn]

"Campuses aren't walled off, though. Any listing can be shared with the whole
network. On Game Day, when Alabama plays at Auburn, Alabama students can see
Auburn students' spare tickets, rides and couches. Under the hood every row has
a tenant ID, and one visibility rule decides what each student sees."


2. PRODUCT VERIFICATION (1:05 - 1:45)
-------------------------------------
[Start creating a listing. Type a price and watch the fair-price check update]

"When a student lists an item, we check it before it goes live. While I type a
price, the fair-price check compares it with similar listings and typical used
prices. If an item is overpriced, or suspiciously cheap, which is a classic
scam sign, it goes to a moderator."

[Try listing a prohibited item, such as a vape or a test bank, and show the rejection]

"Prohibited items like drugs, vapes, weapons, alcohol, fake IDs and test banks
are rejected outright. Buyers only ever see listings that passed these checks."


3. ESCROW (1:45 - 2:30)
-----------------------
[Open a listing with the escrow box and pay into escrow]

"Paying a stranger up front is the biggest risk in student marketplaces, so
CampusLoop sits in the middle, like Upwork. The buyer pays into escrow, and the
seller can see the money is secured but can't touch it yet."

[Show the safe meetup spot, then the Release / Open dispute buttons]

"They meet at a safe campus spot and the buyer inspects the item. If it's good,
they release the payment. If it isn't, they open a dispute."

[Switch to /admin/escrow, the moderator console]

"Disputes come here, where a moderator resolves them. Every step is in an audit
trail. Payment auto-releases after seven days so sellers aren't left waiting,
and there's a 3% buyer-protection fee. Escrow changes run inside database
transactions, so two buyers can never pay for the same item."


4. CHAT SECURITY (2:30 - 3:15)
------------------------------
[Open a chat and send a normal message, then try sending a phone number]

"Every chat message is screened on the server before the other person sees it.
Rules run first and catch phone numbers, emails, card numbers, and tricks like
spelling words with spaces or numbers."

[Send something subtle, like "be careful walking home alone tonight", and show the caution]

"Then an open-weight AI model, OpenAI's gpt-oss-120b running on Groq, judges
what the message means. That's how it catches a veiled threat like this one,
and how it lets through harmless messages a keyword filter would wrongly block.
A blocked message is never delivered, and five blocks in 24 hours pauses that
student's messaging."

[Briefly show /admin/safety and a profile's trust score]

"Messages the AI isn't sure about are delivered but queued for a moderator. All
of this feeds a trust score from 0 to 100, built only from real behavior, with
every point explained on the student's profile."


5. TRADE LOOP (3:15 - 4:10)
---------------------------
[Open the Loops page with the Ana -> Cara -> Ben loop]

"Now the core idea. Most students don't need cash. They need to swap. But a
direct swap only works if two people want each other's items, which rarely
happens. CampusLoop looks further."

"Students list what they have and what they want. We build a graph of who could
give what to whom and look for cycles of two to four people. Kidney-exchange
programs use the same structure to chain donors together."

[Walk through the loop on screen]

"Here's a real one. Ana at Alabama gives her chem book to Cara at Auburn. Cara
gives her Iron Bowl ticket to Ben. Ben gives his mini fridge back to Ana. Three
students, two campuses, everyone gets what they wanted, and zero dollars change
hands."

[Accept the loop and show the group chat that opens]

"Once every member accepts, the loop locks in and a group chat opens, with the
same safety screening and trust scores. The matcher runs again whenever anyone
lists or wants something, so new loops keep appearing."


CLOSE (4:10 - 4:30)
-------------------
[Back to the landing page, or the README logo]

"That's CampusLoop: verified students, verified listings, protected payments,
safe conversations, and trade loops that let students trade in circles across
every campus. It's live now at campusloop-gold.vercel.app. Thanks for watching!"


TIPS FOR RECORDING
------------------
- Do the risky steps, like the blocked messages, the prohibited listing and the
  dispute, once before you record. Then you know they work on the live site,
  and the AI screen isn't rate-limited at the moment you show it.
- If you run long, cut the trust-score line in section 4 and the audit-trail
  line in section 3 first.
- The trade loop is your strongest moment, so slow down there, even if it costs
  time somewhere else.
